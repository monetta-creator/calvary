Calvary Grantmaker Web

Data dictionary

Data dictionary and definitions

Who is in the web

Calvary
Calvary Women's Services, Washington DC (EIN 52-1307706).
Peer organization
One of 18 DMV nonprofits doing work close to Calvary's, chosen as its comparison set. The set is provisional and can still change. All 19 organizations, with what each does, are listed in the side panel; how the peers were chosen is in Methodology.
Grantmaker
An organization that reported at least one grant to Calvary or a peer in its own IRS return, tax years 2020–24. Grantmakers are identified by EIN. Individuals, companies giving directly and government agencies do not file these grant lists, so they do not appear.

Grantmaker types

Private foundation
IRS private-foundation status (files a 990-PF), for example Cafritz or Abell.
Corporate foundation
A private foundation tied to a company, by its name or a known company link, for example Bank of America or JPMorgan Chase.
Community foundation
IRS activity code T31, for example the Greater Washington Community Foundation.
Donor-advised fund sponsor
On the project's list of national donor-advised fund sponsors. Its grants are recommended by individual donors, so it is a route to those donors rather than a grantmaker deciding on its own.
Federated giving
IRS activity code T70: United Way and workplace giving campaigns.
Public charity grantmaker
Any other public charity that lists grants on Schedule I.
Other grantmaker
A filer that fits none of the types above.

Measures on the page

Revenue
An organization's total revenue in its FY2024 Form 990 (the year shown if a later one was used).
Government share
Government grants (990 Part VIII line 1e) divided by total revenue, averaged over FY2022–24. Calvary's FY2022–23 figures use its audited statements. Line 1e can miss government contracts booked as program revenue, so some organizations' shares read low.
Grant
One line in a grantmaker's 990-PF grants-paid list or 990 Schedule I.
Tax year (TY)
The grantmaker's tax year for the return that reports the grant. 2020–24 is used because most 2025 returns are not filed yet.
Granted, amount
Cash and non-cash grants as filed, added up over TY2020–24, in dollars of the day (not adjusted for inflation). Lines that cover several years at once are left out.
Funding link
A grantmaker–organization pair with at least one grant in TY2020–24. Drawn as a line; its width shows the dollars.
Organizations funded
How many of the 19 (Calvary and 18 peers) a grantmaker gave to. Sets the circle's size and the slider.
Peers
In lists, how many of the 18 peers a grantmaker gave to.
Funds Calvary
At least one grant to Calvary appears in the grantmaker's returns for TY2020–24.
Not yet
The grantmaker gives to peers but no grant to Calvary appears in its returns. Calvary may still have received a gift that is not itemized there, such as a recent gift not yet filed.
Bars 2020 → 2024
Dollars by tax year for one grantmaker–organization pair. Bars in the same list share one scale; hover a row for the exact figures.
Winding down
A status note from the engagement's independent review. It currently applies only to the Clark Charitable Foundation.
Methodology

Methodology and sources

What the data is

Grants that grantmakers reported on their own federal tax returns: the grants-paid list on Form 990-PF (private foundations) and Schedule I of Form 990 (public charities, including donor-advised fund sponsors). We read the e-filed returns from the IRS's public e-file data and the GivingTuesday 990 data lake for tax years 2020–24, and kept every grant whose recipient is Calvary or one of the 18 peers. Data as of .

Whose returns were read

  • Every private foundation in DC, Maryland and Virginia.
  • DC, Maryland and Virginia public charities that make grants.
  • National donor-advised fund sponsors such as Fidelity, Schwab, Benevity, National Philanthropic Trust and Vanguard, for their grants to DC, Maryland and Virginia recipients.
  • Grantmakers already known to support Calvary.

How the 18 peers were chosen

Candidates came from 36 DC-area organizations known to serve homeless women, domestic-violence survivors, people in recovery or Wards 7 and 8, plus a search of DMV public charities with revenue of $750K or more and a shelter or domestic-violence code or a women-focused name. Each was scored on women focus, domestic violence, homelessness and housing, recovery, Ward 7/8 and DC location, and closeness to Calvary's size, using its latest e-filed 990, and its filing had to describe homelessness or domestic-violence work. Final picks combine the score with judgment about how similar the program model is.

How the numbers are built

  • Grants are matched to Calvary or a peer by the recipient's EIN when the return gives one, otherwise by exact name with city or state, otherwise by a close name match in the same city.
  • Amounts are as filed, cash and non-cash, added up by grantmaker and organization over the five tax years in dollars of the day. Lines that cover several years at once are left out.
  • A grantmaker's tax year can differ from the recipient's fiscal year, so yearly figures will not line up exactly with an organization's own books.
  • The totals match chart m6_03 and table m6_01 in the analysis (tables fact_grants and dim_funder); the page build checks this before it writes the page.

What these filings do not show

  • Government funding. For Calvary that was of revenue, FY2022–24 average.
  • Individual donors, event income, and companies that give directly rather than through a foundation. None of these file grant lists.
  • Grants a return lists only as "see attached", and returns not filed yet: some 2024 returns are still arriving, and 2025 is left out.
  • Two grantmakers left out on purpose: the Gates Foundation, a national funder whose DC recipients here are research and global-development groups, which distorts totals; and The Community Partnership, DC's homelessness pass-through, whose grants are public money counted elsewhere in the analysis.
  • The Los Angeles comparators, DWC and SheWorks. Only DMV grantmakers' returns were read, so their funders would be missing.

Read with care

  • The peer set is provisional.
  • A grant to a peer shows that a grantmaker funds this kind of work. It does not mean the grantmaker takes applications or would fund Calvary.
  • The "winding down" note on Clark Charitable Foundation comes from the engagement's independent review.
