# The funding-pressure verdict

Draft for CP2, 2026-10-10, revised 2026-10-11 in the review fix cycle (`reports/review/`). Evidence in the module readouts (M1–M3, M9) and the insight register; every figure traces to a chart-data CSV.

## Verdict in one paragraph

Calvary is under **real funding pressure, but not because its government share is unusual**. Government supplies 53% of revenue (FY2022–24 average on the audit basis; 58% in FY25 on the 990), which is mid-pack among DC peers (11th of 19) and at the 57th percentile among DC human-services organizations that report government revenue; against all filers (no government line counted as 0%) Calvary is in the top quarter (84th in DC, 74th nationally). The pressure is structural: (1) the government money arrives through **three channels, two of them via a single intermediary** (TCP carries the HUD CoC renewals and the DHS contract, about three-quarters of Calvary's recurring award base: 78% of the $4.1M 2024 award base, chart m2_01); (2) **private revenue is flat and concentrated**: filed institutional grants have stayed near $1M a year from the same 26–29 funders, and one funder (GWCF, including the time-limited Health Equity Fund) is 27% of five years of filed grants, while event receipts held near $0.53–0.60M (FY24 $596K, FY25 $533K; the FY25 990 presented gala receipts as fundraising-event income, line 8a with $259K of direct costs netted, instead of contributions, line 1c); (3) **FY25 ran a deficit**: −$607K, with cash at 4.4 months of expenses (7.5 in FY21), and at the FY25 run-rate cash lasts about 41 months with no shock at all. The DC budget lines behind Calvary's local money are flat to rising through FY2027, but the Mayor proposed cuts to victim-services and DV lines in each of the last two springs and the Council restored them; the FY2027 OVSJG restoration is $5.1M one-time plus $0.5M recurring, so the FY2028 base is in play again. The local risk is annual and political, not structural. The federal side is the larger uncertainty: the FY2025 CoC NOFO was enjoined and FY2025 renewals were paid in full, so the live question is the FY2026 CoC NOFO (protected renewals 60% of demand, a $1.3B transitional-housing and services set-aside, applications closed 30 September 2026, awards expected about December 2026, litigation pending), and HUD's 29 September 2026 notice ends the EHV program (families leased on 30 September 2026 convert to non-reissuable tenant-protection vouchers by 31 December 2026, so Sisters Rise's voucher base cannot be rebuilt). Both point the same way: the TCP channel is the cloud.

**Rating: moderate-to-high pressure, driven by channel concentration and liquidity rather than by the government share.**

## Scenario results (FY25 baseline, ranges across three splits of government revenue)

Each loss is a range across three ways of splitting FY25 recognized government revenue across streams (ceiling share; CoC at its audited FY22–23 draw rate with DHS the residual; ceiling share without Sisters Rise). The net view assumes half of a lost stream's revenue is avoidable cost. Every loss adds to the FY25 deficit of −$607K.

| Scenario | Revenue loss (range) | Months of cash after a year (net) |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline FY25 | – | 3.1 |
| OVSJG −10% (the FY26 proposed cut) | −$67K to −$100K | 3.0–3.1 |
| OVSJG −25% | −$168K to −$250K | 2.9–3.0 |
| HUD CoC renewals −15% | −$99K to −$207K | 2.9–3.0 |
| One CoC project lost (New Foundations 2) | −$204K to −$424K | 2.7–2.9 |
| DHS/TCP contract not renewed | about −$0.65M to −$1.13M (13–23% of revenue) | 1.9–2.4 |
| Combined (OVSJG −25%, CoC −15%, DHS lost) | about −$1.05M to −$1.47M (21–30% of revenue) | 1.5–2.0 |

Reading: the cuts that make headlines (OVSJG) are survivable; the loss of the TCP-channel contract is not without new money or cost cuts, and the baseline already runs a deficit. The DHS point estimate depends on how revenue splits across streams: a floor case with OVSJG and CoC fully drawn nearly removes it. The headline stands: **the TCP channel is the cloud** (CoC and DHS together are $1.8–2.2M under every split).

## What would change the verdict
- Calvary's FY24 and FY25 audits and its TCP subgrant agreements: they settle the ceilings-vs-recognized gap (D1) and the Sisters Rise question (D2). The audited FY22–23 single audits already place the gap in the CoC stream, so reconcile CoC draw-down first, then DHS. If awards are being under-drawn, the first recommendation becomes "draw what is awarded".
- Flag D3 (event income) is closed, resolved from the 990 itself: receipts held near $0.53–0.60M and the FY25 change was one of presentation, so the private trend is flat rather than falling.
- A change in the core-peer set (provisional) would move the rank (swapping three provisional peers moves Calvary from 11th to 12th of 19) but not the sector percentile.
