# Strategic recommendations (draft for CP2)

Built from the verdict (`funding_pressure_verdict.md`), the scored pipeline (`funder_pipeline.csv`), the 90-day plan (`action_plan_90day.csv`) and modules M1–M10. Targets below are proposals for Calvary's leadership to set, not forecasts.

## The goal in numbers

Move private revenue from about $2.1M (FY25 non-government revenue) to $3.0M within three years while holding the government base, which takes the government share from 58% to roughly 50% at flat government revenue, restores a surplus, and rebuilds cash to a six-month reserve ($2.8M at FY25 expenses). The components: institutional upgrades and new foundations +$0.5M; major-donor and DAF giving +$0.3M; events restored +$0.4M (the FY24 level); earned or billable program revenue +$0.1–0.3M (optional, see expansion).

## 1. Protect the government base and draw all of it (now)
- **Draw-down audit (day 30):** reconcile award ceilings to recognized revenue for FY24–FY26 (ceilings $3.6M / $4.3M vs $2.62M / $2.89M recognized). Undrawn awards are the cheapest revenue there is. Needs Calvary's FY24/FY25 audits and TCP subgrant agreements (flag D1).
- **TCP channel:** 74% of the award base passes through TCP. Confirm the DHS transitional-housing contract and the Sisters Rise/EHV status for FY26 (flag D2); ask for multi-year terms where DHS allows; keep a direct line to DHS and the ICH, not only to TCP.
- **CoC renewals:** protect the three renewals in the next competition; prepare a PSH expansion project (Calvary holds 16 PSH beds; the local DHS PSH budget is $145–166M, 25× the DC CoC pool).
- **OVSJG:** continuation funding has grown every year; budget the Mayor's proposed-cut season (April–June) into the advocacy calendar and join the coalition testimony that has restored the line twice.
- **Risk register:** owner, renewal date, funding source (local vs federal), and a trigger for each stream (M2 durability table).

## 2. Rebuild private revenue on three tracks (0–12 months)
- **Upgrade the majors to multi-year, general operating:** GWCF (largest grant to a peer $526K vs $458K to Calvary; HEF is time-limited, so ask for a successor commitment), Cafritz ($250K vs $170K), Abell, Fowler, United Way NCA, Bank of America. The ask is the verdict: a documented three-stream exposure and a thin reserve.
- **Open the foundations that fund Calvary's peers:** Clark Charitable first (5 peers, median grant $250K, $32.8M to DC's sector; no Calvary grant), then Schattner, Cohen, Bernstein, The Washington Home, Weinberg, Meyer, Eugene B. Casey. Introductions run through the peers that share the most funders with Calvary (Miriam's Kitchen, DASH, House of Ruth DC, New Endeavors, My Sister's Place) and through co-funders.
- **Re-engage lapsed funders** (World Bank Community Connections Fund, Many Hands, Jane Bancroft Robinson, Robert M. Fisher, Junior League) and confirm filing-lag cases (Venable Foundation, America's Charities) in the CRM.
- **Fix the event line:** the $483K drop in FY25 event contributions is the largest single private swing; confirm whether the gala moved across the fiscal year and set the FY26 event at the FY24 level.
- **Major donors and DAFs:** peers with Calvary's profile raise 40–50% of filed grants through DAF sponsors (N Street Village 51%, Miriam's Kitchen 41%; Calvary 25%). A named major-gifts program with DAF-friendly asks is the lever here; Fidelity, Benevity and Schwab already route $1.1M to Calvary over five years.
- **Corporate relationships** (MGAC, Capital One, IMF, Latham, Frontier Co-op, TD Bank) are a larger share of Calvary's private money than filings show ($2.6M of the CRM's $10.6M has no public filing). Quantify cash vs in-kind and convert the strongest into multi-year sponsorships.

## 3. Hold a reserve and stop the burn (0–6 months)
- Set a board reserve policy at six months of expenses (Doorways, N Street Village and Everyone Home DC hold 8–12; the government-anchored DV providers hold about one).
- Close the FY25 operating gap: the deficit was $607K on $5.58M of expenses; with private revenue flat, the choice is between a cost plan and the private tracks above. Model both in the FY26 budget.
- Treat HEF and any other time-limited private source as one-time in the budget.

## 4. Mission-expansion options (6–24 months), with trade-offs
| Option | Distance | Pool evidence | Upside | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formalize workforce** (SheWorks-style cohort inside Step Up DC: stipends, childcare, transport, employer and health-plan partners) | near | $30M/yr DMV tagged; DOES/WIOA; SNAP E&T already held | New funders and public contracts; strong outcomes story | Program staff; partner management |
| **Behavioral-health credential** (DBH certification or a billing partner) | near | $21M/yr tagged; Medicaid | Converts therapy from cost to program-service revenue (Samaritan Inns' path) | Compliance, staffing, 12–18 months |
| **DV program formalization** (VOCA eligibility) | near | OVSJG $55M pool; DV peers 70–89% government | Deepens the OVSJG relationship; opens DV funders | Reporting burden; raises government share |
| **Health partnership** (FQHC clinic hours on site; Community of Hope is a Ward 8 peer; DWC/JWCH model) | medium | $84M/yr tagged, 206 funders at $100K+ | Opens health funders at low capital cost | Partner dependence |
| **Older-women track in PSH** | medium | $9.7M/yr; The Washington Home | Narrative fit; small pool | Small |
| Women veterans / reentry | medium | small private pools; federal (HVRP, BJA) | Program-specific federal money | New compliance regimes |
| Families / maternal | far | $4M/yr | – | Mission change; not recommended |

Recommended sequence: workforce formalization and the behavioral-health credential decision first (both are near and open earned or public revenue), the health partnership second, DV formalization only if the board accepts a higher government share.

## 5. Risk mitigation for the government exposure
- Diversify *within* government: a direct DHS relationship alongside TCP; HUD CPF renewal; DHS PSH expansion; keep federal pass-through money identifiable for single-audit purposes.
- Contract terms: push for multi-year and advance-payment terms where DC allows; track draw-down monthly against ceilings.
- Advocacy calendar: the Mayor's budget (March–April), Council committee markups (May–June), Council vote (June–July); Calvary's lines have been restored twice because peers testified.
- Early-warning indicators: OVSJG approved-vs-actual spending gap (9–14% under-spend), TCP's DHS purchase orders (fell sharply in FY2025 after DC's financial-system change), CoC NOFO priorities, EHV sunset.

## What this is not
Not a recommendation to shrink government revenue: the peers that grew fastest did so on both models. The recommendation is to make government money fully drawn and better diversified across channels, and to grow private money faster than government so the share drifts toward half.
