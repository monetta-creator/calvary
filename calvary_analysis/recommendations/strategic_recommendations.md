# Strategic recommendations (draft for CP2)

Built from the verdict (`funding_pressure_verdict.md`), the scored pipeline (`funder_pipeline.csv`), the 90-day plan (`action_plan_90day.csv`) and modules M1–M10. Targets below are proposals for Calvary's leadership to set, not forecasts.

## The goal in numbers

Move private revenue from about $2.1M (FY25 non-government revenue) to $3.0M within three years while holding the government base, which takes the government share from 58% to roughly 50% at flat government revenue, restores a surplus, and rebuilds cash to a six-month reserve ($2.8M at FY25 expenses). The arithmetic: about $2.1M plus institutional upgrades and peer-funder introductions +$0.5M, major-donor and DAF giving +$0.3M, and earned or billable program revenue +$0.1–0.3M (optional, see expansion), which is +$0.9–1.1M. Events are not a component: receipts held near $0.5M, so there is nothing to restore.

## 1. Protect the government base and draw all of it (now)
- **Draw-down audit (day 30): reconcile CoC draw-down first (the audited years already show the gap there), then DHS.** FY24 / FY25 ceilings were $3.6M / $4.3M against $2.62M / $2.89M recognized, and the audited FY22 and FY23 single audits already show CoC expenditure at 28% and 58% of the prior year's CoC award ceilings (the 2021 Reach Up expansion award, $1.22M reallocated to $0.42M in 2022, explains part). Undrawn awards are the cheapest revenue there is. The DHS step needs Calvary's FY24/FY25 audits and TCP subgrant agreements (flag D1, medium confidence).
- **TCP channel:** about three-quarters of the award base (78% of the $4.1M 2024 award base) passes through TCP, and CoC and DHS together are $1.8–2.2M under every split in the stress test. Confirm the DHS transitional-housing contract for FY26 and plan Sisters Rise without EHV: HUD's 29 September 2026 notice ends the program, so Sisters Rise's voucher base cannot be rebuilt (flag D2). Ask for multi-year terms where DHS allows; keep a direct line to DHS and the ICH, not only to TCP.
- **CoC renewals:** the FY2025 CoC NOFO was enjoined and FY2025 renewals were paid in full; the live question is the FY2026 CoC NOFO (awards expected about December 2026, litigation pending). Protect the three renewals in it; prepare a PSH expansion project for the next competition (Calvary holds 16 PSH beds; the local DHS PSH budget is $145–166M, 25× the DC CoC pool).
- **OVSJG:** continuation funding has grown every year; budget the Mayor's proposed-cut season (April–June) into the advocacy calendar and join the coalition testimony that has restored the line twice. The FY2027 restoration is $5.1M one-time plus $0.5M recurring, so the FY2028 base is in play again in the next spring budget season.
- **Risk register:** owner, renewal date, funding source (local vs federal), and a trigger for each stream (M2 durability table).

## 2. Rebuild private revenue on three tracks (0–12 months)
- **Capacity sets the pace:** Calvary held its fundraising staff at six positions with limited growth over 2021–24 (Calvary's 2021–24 strategic plan report). The 90-day plan is sequenced for that team: month 1 government confirmations (CEO and board chair), month 2 two current-funder conversations a fortnight (development director), month 3 introductions.
- **Deepen the current majors, funder by funder:** Cafritz ($170K to Calvary vs $250K largest to a peer) and Fowler ($40K vs $50K) fit a multi-year general-operating grant at the funder's peer-level amount. **GWCF: two conversations**, a successor to the Health Equity Fund support behind its $458K, and a core general-operating request. **Abell: steward and renew.** Its largest grant to Calvary was $270K (TY2021) and its latest filed grant $25K (TY2023), and Calvary's records show a further $25K in March 2025; Abell is invitation-only with no consecutive-year requests, so ask its staff when a next request is welcome and what it would fund. United Way NCA follows; Bank of America is a matching-gift channel with a corporate-relationship path. The case behind each ask is the verdict: a documented concentration in three public streams, a $607K FY25 deficit and 4.4 months of cash.
- **Open the foundations that fund Calvary's peers, by introduction:** Schattner funds four of Calvary's peers ($800K, typical grant $60K) and is the one new foundation in Tier 1. It does not consider unsolicited requests, so the step is to secure an invitation through a named peer or co-funder; no LOI. Two smaller family foundations also fund four peers (Bunting, Bechtel) but give little elsewhere in DC, publish no application process and sit in Tier 3, so they are not plan targets. Twelve more grantmakers fund exactly three peers each and **depend on the provisional peer set**; Cohen is invitation-only and the rest are not yet status-checked, so confirm the peer set with Calvary and check each funder's own page before outreach. Clark Charitable has closed (its own FAQ: sunsetting, operational until 31 December 2025, no unsolicited proposals); its $7.8M to five peers and $32.8M to DC's sector in TY2020–24 were a spend-down. Meyer closed to new applicants in 2026. Introductions run through the peers that share the most funders with Calvary (Miriam's Kitchen, DASH, House of Ruth DC, New Endeavors, My Sister's Place) and through co-funders.
- **Re-engage lapsed funders:** 11 once gave $0.16M in total; start with Jane Bancroft Robinson, Robert M. Fisher and the Junior League. Many Hands, the largest, requires an operating budget of $500K–$5M; Calvary spent $5.58M in FY2025, so it is not eligible for the January 2027 cycle unless both FY2026 actual expenses and the FY2027 budget fall to $5M. The World Bank Community Connections Fund is not lapsed: its one filed grant was a one-time $100K capital-campaign grant (TY2022), and staff giving through the fund continued to July 2026, so treat it as an employee-giving channel.
- **Channels already paying Calvary:** America's Charities is a workplace-giving federation, not a grantmaker; confirm Calvary's enrollment in the CFC and employer campaigns and its designation code. Venable accepts DC applications only from grantees of the last two years or invited organizations; check in the CRM whether Calvary's recent gifts came from the Foundation rather than the firm.
- **Confirm the FY26 gala date:** event receipts held near $0.53–0.60M (FY24 $596K, FY25 $533K); the FY25 990 presented gala receipts as fundraising-event income (line 8a, with $259K of direct costs netted) instead of contributions (line 1c), and the 2026 gala raised over $620K. The event line needs a date check, not a rebuild.
- **Major donors and DAFs:** peers with Calvary's profile raise 40–50% of filed grants through DAF sponsors (N Street Village 51%, Miriam's Kitchen 41%; Calvary 25%). A named major-gifts program with DAF-friendly asks is the lever here; Benevity, Fidelity and Schwab already route $0.95M to Calvary over five years ($1.1M across all eight DAF sponsors).
- **Corporate relationships** (MGAC, Capital One, IMF, Latham, Frontier Co-op, TD Bank) are a larger share of Calvary's private money than filings show ($2.6M of the CRM's $10.6M has no public filing). Quantify cash vs in-kind and convert the strongest into multi-year sponsorships.

## 3. Hold a reserve and stop the burn (0–6 months)
- Set a board reserve policy at six months of expenses (Doorways, N Street Village and Everyone Home DC hold 8–12; the government-anchored DV providers hold about one).
- Close the FY25 operating gap: the deficit was $607K on $5.58M of expenses; with private revenue flat, the choice is between a cost plan and the private tracks above. Model both in the FY26 budget.
- Treat HEF and any other time-limited private source as one-time in the budget.

## 4. Mission-expansion options (6–24 months), with trade-offs
| Option | Distance | Pool evidence | Upside | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formalize workforce** (SheWorks-style cohort inside Step Up DC: stipends, childcare, transport, employer and health-plan partners) | near | $30M/yr DMV tagged; DOES/WIOA; SNAP E&T already held | New funders and public contracts; strong outcomes story | First cost: funding Calvary's second workforce position, unfunded in FY2026; then program staff and partner management |
| **Behavioral-health credential** (DBH certification or a billing partner) | near | $21M/yr tagged; Medicaid | Converts therapy from cost to program-service revenue (Samaritan Inns' path) | Compliance, staffing, 12–18 months |
| **DV program formalization** (VOCA eligibility) | near | OVSJG $55M pool; DV peers 70–89% government | Deepens the OVSJG relationship; opens DV funders | Reporting burden; raises government share |
| **Health partnership** (FQHC clinic hours on site; Community of Hope is a Ward 8 peer; DWC/JWCH model) | medium | $84M/yr tagged, 206 funders at $100K+ | Opens health funders at low capital cost | Relies on one partner |
| **Older-women track in PSH** | medium | $9.7M/yr; The Washington Home | Narrative fit; small pool | Small |
| Women veterans / reentry | medium | small private pools; federal (HVRP, BJA) | Program-specific federal money | New compliance regimes |
| Families / maternal | far | $4M/yr | – | Mission change; not recommended |

Recommended sequence: workforce formalization (starting with funding the second workforce position) and the behavioral-health credential decision first (both are near and open earned or public revenue), the health partnership second, DV formalization only if the board accepts a higher government share.

## 5. Risk mitigation for the government streams
- Diversify *within* government: a direct DHS relationship alongside TCP; HUD CPF renewal; DHS PSH expansion; keep federal pass-through money identifiable for single-audit purposes.
- Contract terms: push for multi-year and advance-payment terms where DC allows; track draw-down monthly against ceilings, CoC first.
- Advocacy calendar: the Mayor's budget (March–April), Council committee markups (May–June), Council vote (June–July); Calvary's lines have been restored twice because peers testified, and the one-time FY2027 OVSJG restoration puts the FY2028 base back on that calendar.
- Early-warning indicators: OVSJG approved-vs-actual spending gap (9–14% under-spend), TCP's DHS purchase orders (fell sharply in FY2025 after DC's financial-system change), FY2026 CoC NOFO awards (expected about December 2026, litigation pending), the EHV conversion to tenant-protection vouchers by 31 December 2026.

## What this is not
Not a recommendation to shrink government revenue: the peers that grew fastest did so on both models. The recommendation is to make government money fully drawn and better diversified across channels, and to grow private money faster than government so the share drifts toward half.
