# External reality check — public-records funding analysis (data as of 2026-10-10)

Retrieved 2026-10-10. Web search and page fetches only; no bulk downloads. Source IDs refer to `external_sources.csv` in this folder. Several Street Sense and congress.gov pages returned 403 to the fetcher and were read through a plain-text proxy of the same URL; the URL cited is the original.

Verdict key: CONFIRM = public pages support the claim as written. QUALIFY = claim holds but needs a date, figure or caveat. CONTRADICT = public pages say otherwise. NOT FOUND = no public page located.

## 1. GWCF Health Equity Fund — remaining life, size, 2026–27 rounds, successor

**Claim:** "time-limited fund; ask about a successor."

**Found**
- Size and origin: $95M, created March 2022 from the DC Government / GHMSI (CareFirst) settlement MOU; largest fund in the Community Foundation's history (hef_1, hef_6).
- Deployment pace: $51.3M granted by Oct 17 2024 (hef_5); $68M by Dec 18 2025 (hef_2); plus $15.7M multiyear demonstration-project commitments announced Jan 21 2026 (hef_3). That implies roughly $84M of $95M committed by early 2026, leaving on the order of $10M uncommitted (my arithmetic, not a GWCF statement).
- Recent rounds relevant to homelessness: Dec 2025 "Meeting the Moment Through Collective Action" ($5.4M) funded health-safety-net and homelessness collaboratives (Way Home Campaign group led by Miriam's Kitchen, incl. Friendship Place, Pathways to Housing DC, Everyone Home DC) — collaborative/coalition grants, not single-organization operating grants (hef_2).
- 2026–27 rounds: the GWCF Health Equity news feed through June 2026 shows no new open RFP, no wind-down notice, no sunset date and no successor fund (hef_4). The only "2027 deadline" found is a third-party aggregator listing (Instrumentl) whose meta text says "next deadline May 31 2027" while the listing itself is archived as not accepting applications (hef_7) — treat as noise.
- No first-party statement of an end date was found. Earlier GWCF language described a five-year horizon for the fund's strategies (Oct 2024, hef_5), consistent with roughly 2022–2027.

**Verdict: QUALIFY.** "Time-limited" is right (settlement corpus, ~88% committed, five-year framing), but no public wind-down or successor announcement exists as of Oct 2026, so the analysis should say "no successor announced; most recent round (Dec 2025) was coalition-only" rather than imply one is pending. Confidence: medium-high on size/pace; medium on remaining balance (derived).

## 2. HUD Continuum of Care — FY2025 NOFO, FY2026 NOFO, DC-500

**Claim:** "HUD's 2025 CoC NOFO shifted priorities"; CoC renewal risk rated medium.

**Found — FY2025**
- NOFO released Nov 13 2025, $3.918B; capped permanent housing at 30% of a CoC's Annual Renewal Demand; cut protected ("Tier 1") renewals from ~90% to ~30%; redirected funds to transitional housing and services-only projects; HUD's own fact sheet frames it as moving "away from 'Housing First'" with PSH "limited to elderly and disabled" and a treatment/recovery focus (coc_1, coc_2, coc_7).
- Litigation: HUD withdrew the NOFO Dec 8 2025 after suit; D.R.I. written preliminary injunction Dec 23 2025 ordered HUD to restore the FY24-25 competition; HUD reopened the FY24-25 NOFO Jan 9–Feb 9 2026 to process renewals under prior terms; First Circuit left the injunction in place Apr 1 2026 (coc_3, coc_4, coc_5).
- Outcome: Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026 (P.L. 119-75, Feb 3 2026) directed noncompetitive renewal of FY2025 grants. HUD announced renewals in three tranches: Mar 31 2026 ($349.2M, 622 projects), Apr 27 2026 ($1.095B, 1,826), May 21 2026 ($2.403B, 4,241) — $3.85B total, i.e. FY2025 renewals were ultimately protected, not reduced (coc_6, coc_10).

**Found — FY2026**
- NOFO released Jun 1 2026 (modified Jul 24), $4.04B; Tier 1 protected renewals = 60% of ARD (down from ~90% historically, but above FY2025's proposed 30%); $1.3B set-aside for new transitional housing and SSO projects; treatment/recovery scoring raised to up to 20 points; service-participation emphasis replacing Housing First; HUD reserved discretion to award outside the scoring (coc_9, coc_10, coc_11, coc_15). NAEH estimates ~97,000 PH residents at risk nationally (coc_15).
- Litigation: district court set the FY2026 NOFO aside in its entirety Aug 7 2026; First Circuit granted HUD an emergency stay Sept 16 2026; HUD reopened the competition Sept 18 with a Sept 30 2026 8 PM ET deadline; comment period on evidence-based practices closes Oct 13 2026; HUD said awards expected by Dec 1 2026 (coc_8, coc_11, coc_12, coc_13). Merits litigation is unresolved as of Oct 10 2026.

**Found — DC-500**
- TCP's HUD NOFO page confirms DC followed the national timeline: FY26 NOFO released Jun 1 2026, local process paused Aug 7, reopened to meet the Sept 30 deadline; FY25 local materials had been due Dec 12 2025 (coc_14). HUD lists a DC-500 FY2026 Grant Inventory Worksheet (coc_13). No DC priority listing, ranking, or reallocation decisions were found online; TCP posts no renewal outcome.

**Verdict: QUALIFY.** The priority shift is real and documented in HUD's own words, but the sentence should be dated and sequenced: the FY2025 shift was enjoined and FY2025 renewals were paid in full under prior terms; the live risk is the FY2026 NOFO (60% protected renewals, applications closed Sept 30 2026, awards due ~Dec 2026, litigation pending). "Medium" renewal risk is defensible for a PSH/TH provider in DC-500; the analysis should name FY2026 rather than 2025 as the exposure. Confidence: high on national facts; low on DC-500 specifics (nothing public).

## 3. Emergency Housing Vouchers — sunset timeline; DCHA attrition

**Claim:** "EHV is a sunsetting federal voucher program"; a 24-unit EHV-paired PSH project left the 2025 HIC.

**Found**
- HUD letter Mar 6 2025 and Notice PIH 2025-07 (Mar 26 2025) stopped new EHV issuances after 14 days (DCHA stopped Apr 9 2025); PIH 2025-19 (Jun 20 2025) said funds would cover families "through most of CY 2026" and told PHAs to plan wind-down and HCV absorption (ehv_1, ehv_4).
- PIH 2026-25 (Sept 29 2026): the FY2027 continuing resolution (P.L. 119-103, signed Sept 2 2026) funds Tenant Protection Vouchers for every EHV family under lease on Sept 30 2026; conversion to "EHV-TPVs" must finish by Dec 31 2026, after which "the EHV program will end"; TPVs cannot be reissued on turnover, so the stock attrits permanently (ehv_2).
- National attrition: ~59,000 leased Apr 2025 vs 47,000+ Apr 15 2026 (ehv_3).
- DC: 588 EHV households (CBPP, cited Jul 2025) → 517 families cited Jul 1 2026 (about −12% in a year); DCHA said only that it was "exploring funding options and alternative affordable housing options" (ehv_4, ehv_5). The DC Council's FY2027 budget placed $9M on a contingency list for DCHA "if federal emergency voucher funding expires" (dhs_5). No DCHA EHV-specific public notice newer than 2021 was found.
- The 24-unit HIC project drop cannot be checked from the web; nothing public mentions it.

**Verdict: CONFIRM (with update).** Sunset is now concrete: program ends Dec 31 2026 with families converted to non-reissuable TPVs. Add the Sept 29 2026 notice and the 588→517 DC count. The HIC-project claim remains unverified externally. Confidence: high.

## 4. DC FY2027 budget — OVSJG Victim Services grants

**Claim:** approved $55.4M FY26, $55.3M FY27; Mayor proposed −$5.8M (FY26) and −$5.6M (FY27); Council restored both.

**Found (CFO budget books, $ thousands, activity P02303 Victim Services Grants)**
- FY2026 cycle: FY2025 approved 55,922 → Mayor's FY2026 proposed 50,079 (−5,842); FY2026 approved 55,357 (ovsjg_2, ovsjg_1). → −$5.8M proposed, restored to $55.4M.
- FY2027 cycle: Committee pre-hearing questions (Apr 29 2026) cite a "$5,596,000 and 2 FTEs cut to Victim Services" in the Mayor's proposal; OVSJG testimony May 6 2026 put local Victim Services at $43M in the proposal (ovsjg_3, ovsjg_4). FY2027 approved book: P02303 = 55,331, Victim Services program = 55,500; Table FO0-5 shows Council enhancements of +$5,100K (one-time) and +$500K (recurring) "to support Victim Services grants" (ovsjg_1). → −$5.6M proposed, restored to $55.3M. Legal Aid DC (Jul 13 2026) independently reports the Victim Services cut (~$5.5M) was "fully restored" (ovsjg_5).
- Caveat worth stating: the FY27 restoration is $5.1M one-time + $0.5M recurring, so the FY2028 base is again exposed. Also, the Special Purpose Revenue line in Victim Services fell $2.56M in FY27 (3,157 → 600) "to align with projected revenues"; the grants activity itself is flat.

**Verdict: CONFIRM.** All six figures match the CFO books to within rounding. Suggest adding the one-time nature of the FY27 restoration. Confidence: high.

## 5. DHS homeless services continuum FY2026–27; TCP contract cuts / payment delays

**Claim:** DHS continuum $170M → $206M → $194M; "TCP's DHS purchase orders fell sharply in FY2025 after DC's financial-system change."

**Found (CFO books, $ thousands; sum of Homeless Services Continuum – Families, General, Individuals, Youth)**
- FY2025 actual: 76,669 + 66,502 + 32,133 + 3,501 = 178,805 (dhs_1). The analysis's $170M does not match FY2025 actuals; it may be an FY2025 approved figure from the FY2026 book (not retrieved) or a three-line sum (175,304). Needs a stated basis.
- FY2026 approved: 93,859 + 75,199 + 33,216 + 3,584 = 205,858 → $206M. Match (dhs_1, dhs_2).
- FY2027: Mayor proposed 186,895 (−$18M+, as Street Sense reported Apr 22 2026) (dhs_1, dhs_3); Council-approved 95,566 + 62,076 + 32,717 + 3,390 = 193,749 → $194M. Match (dhs_2). Note the Council shifted money toward PSH-Families (+$25.4M to 86,807) and vouchers ($28.2M for 469 existing vouchers; 190 for RRH exits; final vote Jun 23 2026) while the "General" continuum line still fell $13.1M (dhs_2, dhs_5). DV Services fell to 3,114 (−$185K after Council restored $500K of a $685K cut) (dhs_2, dhs_4).
- Payment delays: documented for FY2025 — DHS grantees unpaid from Oct 1 2024 into late January 2025 for lack of purchase orders or executed contracts; providers attributed it to staff turnover and "a new software system to manage grants and contracts" (dhs_6, dhs_7). TCP is not named in either source, and nothing public describes TCP's own FY2025 purchase-order volume. TCP's site (Oct 2026) shows a provider RFP (proposals due Sept 4 2026) that excludes hypothermia-season procurement, but no payment or cut notice (dhs_8).

**Verdict: QUALIFY.** FY2026 ($206M) and FY2027 approved ($194M) are confirmed to the dollar; FY2025 "$170M" is unsupported by actuals ($178.8M) and needs its source named. The FY2025 payment-delay context is real and dated (Jan 2025, new grants/contracts software), but the TCP purchase-order claim is from the analysis's own data and has no public corroboration; phrase it as an observed data pattern, not a reported event. Confidence: high on budget lines; low on the TCP mechanism.
