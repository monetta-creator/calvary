# Recommendations report

*For the consulting team and Calvary's leadership. Data as of 2026-10-10 (revised 2026-10-11). This report brings together the verdict (`recommendations/funding_pressure_verdict.md`), the scored pipeline (`recommendations/funder_pipeline.csv`), the 90-day plan (`recommendations/action_plan_90day.csv`) and the strategic options (`recommendations/strategic_recommendations.md`). Every figure traces to a chart ID in `chart_data/` and from there to the source registry. Tone: these are options for Calvary's team to weigh, offered with respect for what they have built.*

## 1. The verdict

Calvary is under moderate-to-high funding pressure, driven by **channel concentration and liquidity** rather than by its government share. The share (53% of revenue on the FY22–24 average, 58% in FY25) is typical: 11th of 19 DC-area organizations doing similar work (the stronger evidence) and the 57th percentile among DC human-services organizations that report government revenue; against all filers, with no government line counted as 0%, Calvary is in the top quarter (84th in DC, 74th nationally). What is not typical is the combination of three public streams with two passing through one intermediary (TCP carries about three-quarters: 78% of the $4.1M 2024 award base), a private base that has held steady at about $1M a year in filed grants from the same 26–29 funders (one partner 27%), and an FY25 that closed with a $607K deficit and 4.4 months of cash. Event receipts held near $0.53–0.60M (FY24 $596K, FY25 $533K); the FY25 990 presented gala receipts as fundraising-event income (line 8a, with $259K of direct costs netted) instead of contributions (line 1c).

The clouds: DC's budgets behind Calvary's local money have held flat to rising through FY2027, but the Mayor proposed cuts to the victim-services and DV lines in each of the last two springs (the Council restored both, though the FY2027 OVSJG restoration is $5.1M one-time plus $0.5M recurring, so the FY2028 base is back on the table). The federal side is less predictable. The FY2025 CoC NOFO was enjoined and FY2025 renewals were paid in full; the live question is the FY2026 CoC NOFO (protected renewals 60% of demand, a $1.3B transitional-housing and services set-aside, awards expected about December 2026, litigation pending). HUD's 29 September 2026 notice ends the EHV program, converting leased families to non-reissuable tenant-protection vouchers by 31 December 2026, so Sisters Rise's voucher base cannot be rebuilt. And Calvary recognized only 67–73% of its known award ceilings as revenue in FY24–25. The audited FY22 and FY23 single audits already show CoC expenditure at 28% (FY22) and 58% (FY23) of the prior year's CoC award ceilings, so the gap is specific to the CoC stream and predates FY24 (the 2021 Reach Up expansion award, $1.22M reallocated to $0.42M in 2022, explains part); confidence stays medium.

Scenario results on the FY25 baseline (`m2_07`) are ranges across three ways of splitting FY25 recognized government revenue across streams (ceiling share; CoC at its audited FY22–23 draw rate with DHS the residual; ceiling share without Sisters Rise): OVSJG −10% costs $67K–$100K; OVSJG −25% $168K–$250K; CoC renewals −15% $99K–$207K; one CoC project (New Foundations 2) lost $204K–$424K; the DHS/TCP contract lost about $0.65M–$1.13M a year (13–23% of revenue), leaving 1.9–2.4 months of cash after a year; the combined case about $1.05M–$1.47M (21–30%), leaving 1.5–2.0 months of cash after a year, both with half of the lost costs avoided. The DHS point estimate depends on how revenue splits across streams; a floor case with OVSJG and CoC fully drawn nearly removes it. The headline cuts are survivable; the TCP channel is the cloud, since CoC and DHS together are $1.8–2.2M under every split.

## 4. Strategic options

See `recommendations/strategic_recommendations.md` for the full text. In brief:

1. **Protect and fully draw the public base:** reconcile CoC draw-down first (the audited years already show the gap there), then DHS; TCP channel documented and diversified with a direct DHS line; CoC renewals defended through the FY2026 competition and a PSH expansion scoped; OVSJG budget season on the calendar; a stream risk register.
2. **Rebuild private revenue on three tracks:** funder-specific upgrades with current majors (peer-level multi-year asks to Cafritz and Fowler; two GWCF conversations; steward and renew Abell); an invitation to Schattner, the one new foundation in Tier 1, through a peer that it funds; lapsed funders; a major-gifts and DAF program (peers raise 40–50% of filed grants through DAFs; Calvary 25%); corporate relationships converted to multi-year. Event receipts have held near $0.53–0.60M, so the event line needs no rebuild.
3. **Hold a reserve and stop the burn:** six months of expenses (about $2.8M); close the FY25 gap in the FY26 budget; treat time-limited sources (Health Equity Fund) as one-time.
4. **Expand near first:** workforce formalization (SheWorks-style cohort inside Step Up DC; its first cost is funding Calvary's second workforce position, unfunded in FY2026), a behavioral-health credential or billing partner (the Samaritan Inns path to program revenue), then an FQHC clinic partnership; DV formalization only with the higher public share understood; families/maternal not suggested.
5. **Mitigate government risk within government:** direct DHS relationship alongside TCP; HUD CPF and DHS PSH as channels; contract terms; monthly draw tracking; advocacy calendar; early-warning indicators (OVSJG under-spend, TCP's DHS purchase orders, FY2026 CoC awards, the EHV conversion by 31 December 2026, the FY2028 OVSJG base).

**Target arithmetic:** private revenue from about $2.1M (FY25) to about $3.0M in three years (institutional upgrades and peer-funder introductions +$0.5M, major-donor and DAF giving +$0.3M, earned or billable program revenue +$0.1–0.3M if pursued, +$0.9–1.1M in all; events held near $0.5M, so no restoration component) brings the public share to roughly half at flat public funding, restores a surplus and rebuilds the reserve.

## 5. What would change these recommendations

- Calvary's FY24 and FY25 audits and TCP subgrant agreements (flag D1): the audited FY22–23 years already show the CoC gap; if awards are now being fully drawn, recommendation 1 shrinks to monitoring; if not, it is the largest single lever.
- Confirmation on Sisters Rise / EHV (flag D2): if the stream ended in FY25, the scenario baseline falls and recommendation 2's target rises. HUD's 29 September 2026 notice ends the EHV program, so the voucher base cannot be rebuilt either way.
- The FY25 event question (flag D3) is closed from the 990 itself: receipts held near $0.53–0.60M and the change was in presentation (line 8a instead of line 1c). Only the FY26 gala date remains to confirm.
- A change in the provisional peer set would move the gap list and the rank but not the sector percentile or the verdict: swapping three provisional peers drops several of the twelve funders of exactly three peers and moves Calvary from 11th to 12th of 19.
