# Board readout: talking points

*For the person presenting to Calvary's board. Twelve minutes of talk, then discussion. Every number is on a slide of the leadership deck with its source; the slide numbers refer to `decks/leadership_deck.pptx`. Say it plainly, credit the team, and ask for decisions.*

## Opening (slides 1–3, 2 minutes)

- "This is a pro bono look at Calvary's funding using only public records: IRS filings, funders' own grant lists, DC and federal award records, DC budget books. It is offered as a starting point, not a verdict."
- "First, what you have built. Revenue is up 59% in real terms since FY19. Housing inventory went from 68 beds in 2022 to 124 in 2025: Calvary now provides almost 7% of all transitional-housing beds in the District. Three public partners fund about $4.1M a year of awards. Fifty foundations and donor-advised sponsors made grants in the last five years. All of this in Wards 7 and 8, where poverty is double the city rate."

## The funding picture (slides 4–7, 3 minutes)

- "Public funding is about half of revenue. That is typical: Calvary sits 11th of 19 similar DC organizations and near the middle of the sector. The share is not the issue."
- "The shape is. The money comes through three streams, and two of them, the HUD Continuum of Care renewals and the DHS contract, pass through TCP. TCP carries about three-quarters of the base."
- "Private support has been steady at about a million a year in foundation grants, from the same 26 to 29 funders. The Community Foundation, including the Health Equity Fund, is more than a quarter of it, and that fund is time-limited."
- "FY25 was a hard year after a strong FY24: a $607K deficit, event income down from $538K to $55K, and cash down to 4.4 months. Part of the event drop may be timing; we have a question for you on that."

## The clouds (slides 8–10, 3 minutes)

- "DC's budgets behind Calvary's local money have held through FY2027. The Mayor proposed cuts to victim services in each of the last two springs and the Council restored both. So the local risk is annual and political, something a calendar and a coalition can manage."
- "We tested what cuts would do. The OVSJG cut proposed last year would cost Calvary about $67K. Losing one CoC project, about $366K. Losing the DHS contract through TCP, about $960K, a fifth of revenue. The headline cuts are survivable. The TCP-channel contract is the one to plan for."
- "One more thing to check rather than conclude: Calvary recognized about two-thirds of its known award ceilings as revenue in FY24 and FY25. Awards are not revenue, and the DHS figure is carried forward from one public year, so this may be nothing. Your FY24 and FY25 audits would settle it in an afternoon, and if money is being left undrawn it is the cheapest revenue there is."

## What peers show and where the opportunities are (slides 11–14, 2 minutes)

- "Organizations like Calvary have grown on more than one model. The closest fit is N Street Village's: grow private support alongside a stable public base. Calvary already works its foundation funders harder than most peers; the headroom is bigger multi-year grants, major donors and donor-advised funds, and earned revenue."
- "The money is there. DMV funders put about $132M a year into DC's sector. Twenty-three of them fund three or more of Calvary's peers and not yet Calvary, led by Clark Charitable, which gave five of those peers $7.8M. Calvary shares the most funders with Miriam's Kitchen, DASH and House of Ruth DC; the next funders are one introduction away."

## The plan and the decisions (slides 15–21, 2 minutes)

- "We suggest a 90-day plan on four fronts: draw every awarded dollar; upgrade current partners to multi-year grants; open new foundations through peer introductions; and restore the event line. Eleven Tier 1 targets each have a next step, an owner and a deadline."
- "Two policy choices for the board: a six-month reserve target, about $2.8M, and an advocacy calendar so the spring budget season becomes routine."
- "Two expansion options worth scoping this year: a formal workforce cohort inside Step Up DC, modeled on SheWorks in Los Angeles, and a behavioral-health credential that would let therapy earn program revenue, as Samaritan Inns did."
- "Three documents from Calvary would sharpen every number: the FY24 and FY25 audits, the TCP subgrant agreements, and the FY25 gala calendar."

## Decisions to ask for

1. Does the framing fit what you know? What is wrong or missing?
2. The Tier 1 list and the owners: agree, adjust, add.
3. Which two expansion options to scope.
4. A reserve target and the advocacy calendar on the board's own calendar.
5. The three documents, and a date for the working session.

## If asked

- *"Why is the government share not the problem?"* Peers with Calvary's share grew fine; the fragile part is three streams, one hub and a thin cushion.
- *"Are the peers right?"* They are provisional; swapping them moves the rank, not the sector percentile or the verdict.
- *"How confident are the numbers?"* Five independent sources agree on Calvary's revenue within 1% every year; the government streams come from the funders' own award lists; the weakest spots are the two latest years without public audits and the DHS contract values, which are public for one year.
- *"What about board connections?"* Public filings show one high-confidence overlap, not with a funder; the consulting team has a list of unconfirmed leads. The real paths run through shared funders and peers.
