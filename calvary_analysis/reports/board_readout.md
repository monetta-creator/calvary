# Board readout: talking points

*For the person presenting to Calvary's board. Twelve minutes of talk, then discussion. Every number is on a slide of the leadership deck with its source; the slide numbers are the page numbers of `decks/pdf/leadership_deck.pdf` (26 pages, including four section dividers). Say it plainly, credit the team, and ask for decisions.*

## Opening (slides 1–3, 2 minutes)

- "This is a pro bono look at Calvary's funding using only public records: IRS filings, funders' own grant lists, DC and federal award records, DC budget books. It is offered as a starting point, not a verdict."
- "First, what you have built. Revenue nearly doubled from FY19 to FY25, $2.49M to $4.97M, and is up 59% after inflation. Housing inventory went from 68 beds in 2022 to 124 in 2025 across transitional, permanent supportive and rapid-rehousing programs, and Calvary's 94 transitional beds are almost 7% of the District's. Three public partners fund about $4.1M a year of awards. Fifty foundations and donor-advised sponsors made grants in the last five years. All of this in Wards 7 and 8, where poverty is double the city rate."

## The funding picture (slides 4–7, 3 minutes)

- "Public funding is about half of revenue. That is typical: Calvary sits 11th of 19 similar DC organizations, and near the middle of DC human-services organizations that report government revenue. Counting every filer, including those with no government line, Calvary is in the top quarter, so we lean on the peer rank. The share is not the issue."
- "The shape is. The money comes through three streams, and two of them, the HUD Continuum of Care renewals and the DHS contract, pass through TCP. TCP carries about three-quarters of the base: 78% of the $4.1M 2024 award base."
- "Private support has been steady at about a million a year in foundation grants, from the same 26 to 29 funders. The Community Foundation, including the Health Equity Fund, is more than a quarter of it, and that fund is time-limited."
- "FY25 closed with a $607K deficit and 4.4 months of cash, after a strong FY24. The gala held up: event receipts were $596K in FY24 and $533K in FY25. The FY25 990 shows them as fundraising-event income rather than contributions, which is why the event line looks lower on the return. The 2026 gala raised over $620K."

## The clouds (slides 8–11, 3 minutes)

- "DC's budgets behind Calvary's local money have held through FY2027. The Mayor proposed cuts to victim services in each of the last two springs and the Council restored both. The FY2027 restoration is $5.1M one-time plus $0.5M recurring, so the same question comes back for FY2028. The local risk is annual and political, something a calendar and a coalition can manage."
- "We tested what cuts would do, as ranges, because the answer depends on how revenue splits across streams. The OVSJG cut proposed last year would cost Calvary about $67K to $100K. Losing one CoC project, $204K to $424K. Losing the DHS contract through TCP, about $0.65M to $1.13M, 13 to 23% of revenue. The headline cuts are survivable. The TCP channel is the one to plan for: CoC and DHS together are $1.8M to $2.2M however the split falls. HUD's FY2026 CoC awards and the end of the EHV vouchers behind Sisters Rise sit in the same channel."
- "One more thing to check: Calvary recognized about two-thirds of its known award ceilings as revenue in FY24 and FY25. Awards are not revenue, and the DHS figure is carried forward from one public year. But the audited FY22 and FY23 years already show a gap in the CoC stream, with spending at 28% and 58% of the prior year's ceilings, so we suggest reconciling CoC draw-down first, then DHS. If money is being left undrawn it is the cheapest revenue there is."

## What peers show and where the opportunities are (slides 12–17, 2 minutes)

- "Organizations like Calvary have grown on more than one model. The closest fit is N Street Village's: grow private support alongside a stable public base. Calvary already works its foundation funders harder than most peers; the headroom is bigger multi-year grants, major donors and donor-advised funds, and earned revenue."
- "The money is there. DMV funders put about $132M a year into DC's sector. Twenty-three of them fund three or more of Calvary's peers and not yet Calvary. Six of those are giving channels rather than grantmakers, Clark has closed and Venable is closed to new applicants, which leaves 15 that choose their own grantees. The firmest new name is Schattner, which funds four peers and works by invitation, so the path is an introduction through a peer or co-funder. Calvary shares the most funders with Miriam's Kitchen, DASH and House of Ruth DC; the next funders are one introduction away."

## The plan and the decisions (slides 18–26, 2 minutes)

- "We suggest a 90-day plan on four fronts, sequenced for a fundraising team of six. First, draw every awarded dollar and confirm the government base in month 1. Second, deepen current partners with two conversations a fortnight in month 2. Third, open doors to new foundations through peers in month 3. Fourth, grow individual giving with a named major-gifts and DAF program. Nine Tier 1 targets each have a next step, an owner and a deadline."
- "Two policy choices for the board: a six-month reserve target, about $2.8M, and an advocacy calendar so the spring budget season becomes routine."
- "Two expansion options worth scoping this year: a formal workforce cohort inside Step Up DC, modeled on SheWorks in Los Angeles, whose first cost is funding the second workforce position, unfunded in FY2026; and a behavioral-health credential that would let therapy earn program revenue, as Samaritan Inns did."
- "Two documents from Calvary would sharpen every number: the FY24 and FY25 audits and the TCP subgrant agreements."

## Decisions to ask for

1. Does the framing fit what you know? What is wrong or missing?
2. The Tier 1 list, the owners and the provisional peer set: agree, adjust, add.
3. Which two expansion options to scope.
4. A reserve target and the advocacy calendar on the board's own calendar.
5. The two documents, and a date for the working session.

## If asked

- *"Why is the government share not the problem?"* Peers with Calvary's share grew fine; the fragile part is three streams, one hub and a thin cushion.
- *"Are the peers right?"* They are provisional; swapping three of them moves Calvary from 11th to 12th of 19 and changes some of the funder names, not the sector percentile or the verdict. That is why we ask you to confirm the set before outreach.
- *"How confident are the numbers?"* Five independent sources agree on Calvary's revenue within 1% every year; the government streams come from the funders' own award lists; the weakest spots are the two latest years without public audits, the DHS contract values (public for one year) and how revenue splits across streams; that is why the cut scenarios are ranges.
- *"What about board connections?"* Public filings show no high-confidence board links; the consulting team has a list of unconfirmed leads. The real paths run through shared funders and peers.
