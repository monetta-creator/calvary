# Calvary Women's Services: funding position and opportunities — one page

*Pro bono analysis from public records, data as of 2026-10-10 (revised 2026-10-11). For Calvary's leadership; the full decks and reports carry every method and number.*

## What Calvary has built
Revenue grew from $2.49M (FY19) to $4.97M (FY25) in nominal dollars, +59% after inflation; housing inventory rose from 68 beds (2022) to 124 (2025) across transitional, permanent supportive and rapid-rehousing programs, and its 94 transitional beds are 6.8% of the District's; three public partners (DC OVSJG, HUD Continuum of Care via TCP, DC DHS via TCP) fund about $4.1M a year of awards; 50 foundations and donor-advised sponsors filed grants to Calvary in TY2020–24. All of it in the two wards with the deepest need in the city.

## The verdict, in a sentence
Public funding at about half of revenue is typical for DC organizations doing this work (Calvary ranks 11th of 19 DC-area peers); the thing to plan around is its **shape** (three streams, two through one intermediary, with about three-quarters of the base through TCP: 78% of the $4.1M 2024 award base), a **private base that has held steady at about $1M a year** from the same funders (one of them 27% of the total), and **cash at 4.4 months** after FY25's $607K deficit. Moderate-to-high pressure, driven by concentration and liquidity, not by the share itself.

## The clouds on the horizon
- DC's budgets behind Calvary's local money have held flat to rising through FY2027 and the Council has restored every proposed cut, but the threat returns each spring: the FY2027 OVSJG restoration is $5.1M one-time plus $0.5M recurring, so the FY2028 base is back on the table.
- An OVSJG cut of the size proposed last year costs about $67K–$100K; losing the DHS contract through TCP costs about $0.65M–$1.13M a year (13–23% of revenue, depending on how revenue splits across streams), and the combined case leaves 1.5–2.0 months of cash after a year. The TCP channel is the cloud: CoC and DHS together are $1.8–2.2M under every split.
- Calvary recognized about two-thirds of its known award ceilings as revenue in FY24–25; the audited FY22–23 years already show the gap in CoC (spending at 28% and 58% of prior-year ceilings), so look at CoC draw-down first, then DHS.

## The top opportunities
1. **Draw every awarded dollar**: reconcile CoC draw-down first, then DHS, and confirm the DHS/Sisters Rise contracts with TCP (day 30).
2. **Upgrade current partners, funder by funder**: peer-level multi-year general operating from Cafritz (deadlines Mar 1, Jul 1, Nov 1) and Fowler; two GWCF conversations (a Health Equity Fund successor, core operating support); steward and renew Abell (invitation-only); then United Way NCA and Bank of America (day 45–60).
3. **Open doors through peers**: Schattner funds four of Calvary's peers and works by invitation, so the step is an introduction through a named peer or co-funder (no LOI); confirm the provisional peer set first. Watch for Jane Bancroft Robinson's next RFP (East of the River focus) and re-engage the other lapsed funders, 11 who once gave $0.16M (day 60–90).
4. **Start a major-gifts/DAF program**: peers with Calvary's profile raise 40–50% of filed grants through donor-advised funds (Calvary 25%). Events are steady: receipts have held near $0.53–0.60M a year.
5. **Formalize workforce and behavioral health**: a SheWorks-style cohort in Step Up DC (first cost: the second workforce position, unfunded in FY2026) and a behavioral-health credential open the largest nearby pools ($30M and $21M a year in the DMV) without changing the mission.

## Headline recommendations
- A **six-month reserve target** (about $2.8M) and a plan to close the FY25 gap in the FY26 budget.
- An **annual advocacy and monitoring calendar** for the spring budget season, with monthly draw-down tracking against ceilings.
- A **private-revenue goal of about $3M within three years**, which brings the public share to roughly half at flat public funding.
