# Calvary Women's Services: funding position and opportunities — one page

*Pro bono analysis from public records, data as of 2026-10-10. For Calvary's leadership; the full decks and reports carry every method and number.*

## What Calvary has built
Revenue grew 59% in real terms from FY19 to FY25 ($2.49M → $4.97M); housing inventory rose from 68 beds (2022) to 124 (2025), now 6.8% of DC's transitional-housing beds; three public partners (DC OVSJG, HUD Continuum of Care via TCP, DC DHS via TCP) fund about $4.1M a year of awards; 50 foundations and donor-advised sponsors filed grants to Calvary in TY2020–24. All of it in the two wards with the deepest need in the city.

## The verdict, in a sentence
Public funding at about half of revenue is typical for DC organizations doing this work; the thing to plan around is its **shape** (three streams, two through one intermediary, 74% of the base through TCP), a **private base that has held steady at about $1M a year** from the same funders (one of them 27% of the total), and a **thinner cash cushion** after FY25 (a $607K deficit, event income down $483K, 4.4 months of cash). Moderate-to-high pressure, driven by concentration and liquidity, not by the share itself.

## The clouds on the horizon
- DC's budgets behind Calvary's local money have held flat to rising through FY2027 and the Council has restored every proposed cut, but the threat returns each spring.
- An OVSJG cut of the size proposed last year costs about $67K; losing the DHS contract through TCP costs about $960K (19% of revenue), and the combined worst case leaves under two months of cash after a year.
- Calvary recognized about two-thirds of its known award ceilings as revenue in FY24–25: the first place to look, and one the FY24/FY25 audits would settle.

## The top opportunities
1. **Draw every awarded dollar** and confirm the DHS/Sisters Rise contracts with TCP (day 30).
2. **Upgrade current partners to multi-year general operating**: GWCF (and a Health Equity Fund successor), Cafritz (deadlines Mar 1, Jul 1, Nov 1), Abell, Fowler, United Way NCA, Bank of America (day 45).
3. **Open the foundations that already fund Calvary's peers**: Clark Charitable ($7.8M to five peers, median grant $250K), Schattner, Cohen, Bernstein, The Washington Home; re-engage Many Hands (January window), World Bank CCF, Jane Bancroft Robinson (day 60–90).
4. **Restore the event line and start a major-gifts/DAF program**: peers with Calvary's profile raise 40–50% of filed grants through donor-advised funds (Calvary 25%).
5. **Formalize workforce and behavioral health**: a SheWorks-style cohort in Step Up DC and a behavioral-health credential open the largest nearby pools ($30M and $21M a year in the DMV) without changing the mission.

## Headline recommendations
- A **six-month reserve target** (about $2.8M) and a plan to close the FY25 gap in the FY26 budget.
- An **annual advocacy and monitoring calendar** for the spring budget season, with monthly draw-down tracking against ceilings.
- A **private-revenue goal of about $3M within three years**, which brings the public share to roughly half at flat public funding.
