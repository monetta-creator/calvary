# Leadership and board deck: headline spine (v2, consulting craft applied)

Read top to bottom, these headlines are the deck. Each is a full sentence with a verb, 20 words or fewer, two lines at most, stating the slide's takeaway. The deck opens with an executive summary in Situation–Complication–Resolution form and the sections are the argument. Tone: what Calvary has built first, the clouds on the horizon plainly, then options the team can choose among.

1. Calvary's funding today, and the opportunities ahead *(title)*
2. Calvary has become a system-level provider whose funding needs a better shape and a deeper cushion, not a smaller share. *(executive summary)*
3. In five years Calvary has grown revenue 59% in real terms and its housing inventory to 124 beds.
4. Public partners funded most of that growth and now provide about half of revenue, typical for DC peers.
5. The question is the shape of the public money, since three streams, two through one intermediary, carry $4.1M a year.
6. Private support has held steady near $1M a year in grants, with one partner providing more than a quarter.
7. After a strong FY24, FY25 brought a $607K deficit, a $483K event-income drop, and 4.4 months of cash.
8. DC's budgets behind Calvary's local funding have held through FY2027, but a cut is proposed every spring.
9. Calvary can absorb the headline cuts, but losing the DHS contract through TCP is the cloud to plan for.
10. Calvary recognized about two-thirds of its known award ceilings in FY24–25, the first place to look.
11. Peers show three workable paths, and N Street Village's, growing private support beside a stable public base, fits Calvary best.
12. Calvary already works its foundation funders harder than most peers, so the headroom is deeper grants, donors and earned revenue.
13. DMV funders give DC's sector $132M a year, and 23 of them already fund three or more of Calvary's peers.
14. Calvary sits inside the funder web, so the next funders are one introduction away through peers that share its funders.
15. We suggest a 90-day plan that draws every awarded dollar, upgrades partners, opens new foundations and restores the event line.
16. Eleven Tier 1 targets come with a next step, an owner and a deadline.
17. Formalizing workforce and behavioral health opens the largest nearby pools without changing the mission.
18. A six-month reserve and an annual advocacy calendar would make the spring budget season routine.
19. The Los Angeles comparators show scale with government (DWC) and a program design to borrow (SheWorks).
20. The FY24–25 audits, the TCP agreements and the FY25 gala calendar would sharpen every number in this deck.
21. The next steps are your reactions, the three documents, and a working session that turns Tier 1 into assignments.
22. Every figure traces to a chart-data file and a public source, and the consulting and appendix decks carry the methods. *(closing)*
