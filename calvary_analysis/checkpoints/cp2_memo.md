## 1. The funding-pressure verdict

**Exposed, but not because the government share is unusual.** Government is 53% of revenue (FY2022–24 mean, audit basis; 58% in FY25 on the 990): 12th of 19 DC-area organizations, near the sector median. The exposure is structural:
- **Channel concentration:** three streams (OVSJG $1.05M FY26; HUD CoC via TCP $1.77M; DHS via TCP $1.42M in FY24), and TCP carries 74% of the $4.1M award base.
- **Flat, concentrated private money:** filed grants ~$1M/yr from the same 26–29 funders; GWCF (incl. the time-limited Health Equity Fund) = 27% of five-year filed grants, top five = 63%; event income $538K → $55K in FY25.
- **Thinning cash:** FY25 deficit −$607K, cash 4.4 months (7.5 in FY21); the baseline alone burns cash (~41 months to zero).
- **Pools:** DC's OVSJG and DHS lines are flat to rising through FY2027, with Mayor-proposed cuts restored by the Council in FY26 and FY27. Calvary holds 1.1% of OVSJG and 5.3% of DC CoC awards.

**Scenarios (FY25 baseline):** OVSJG −10% costs $67K; CoC −15% $178K; losing one CoC project $366K; losing the DHS/TCP contract $960K (19% of revenue); combined worst case $1.31M (26%) → about 1.7 months of cash after a year even with 50% cost avoidance. The headline cuts are survivable; a TCP-channel loss is not without new money or cuts.

**Rating: moderate-to-high, driven by channel concentration and liquidity.**
