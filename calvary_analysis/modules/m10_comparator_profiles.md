## Downtown Women's Center (DWC), Los Angeles — EIN 31-1597223, NTEE P83, FY = calendar year

| | FY2014 | FY2018 | FY2021 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.3M | $11.6M | $21.0M | $34.4M |
| Government share (line 1e) | 24% | 31% | 44% | 57% |
| Private contributions | $4.0M | $6.9M | $9.9M | $12.8M |
| Program service revenue | $0.5M | $1.1M | $1.2M | $1.8M |
| Months of cash | 2.1 | 3.1 | 3.1 | 2.4 |

- **Trajectory:** revenue ×5.5 in a decade; government went from a quarter to well over half. Private contributions tripled in absolute terms but fell from 64% to 37% of revenue. Program service revenue (social enterprise, rental) is 5%.
- **Federal money** ($21.6M FY2020–25 in single audits): 99% pass-through, led by HUD CoC 14.267 ($14.3M), ESG 14.231 ($3.5M), VOCA 16.575 ($2.7M), Coronavirus Relief Fund, SNAP E&T, CDBG, EFSP, plus a $194K HUD Community Project Funding award. These are the same federal channels Calvary uses, at about five times the scale, routed through LA County and LAHSA rather than TCP.
- **Programs:** 159 PSH units plus community-based housing (451 people housed in 2024); Skid Row's only women's health clinic (with JWCH); MADE by DWC paid transitional work; Rosa's Place (97 PSH units) under construction. 358 employees, 1,750 volunteers.
- **Funders:** 2023 donor list 202 organizations, 2025 list 148; $1M+ band includes GO-Biz, LA County DHS and LAHSA; announced grants: Hilton $1.53M over two years (2026), Blue Shield of California Foundation $450K (2024), Ahmanson $325K (FY2023), Anthem Blue Cross Foundation $200K (2023) and $300K (2026), LA County First District $3M (2025). DMV funders in the data: Bank of America Charitable Foundation ($154K over four years), Wells Fargo ($100K), Give Lively, ImpactAssets, Bainum, JPMorgan Chase. DAF grants to DWC are not captured (Schedule I kept for DC/MD/VA recipients).
- **What transfers to Calvary:** (1) the housing-development pipeline, using CoC and county PSH money to own and operate units; (2) the health-clinic partnership model with a community health provider; (3) a paid transitional-work enterprise (MADE) as a workforce program with earned revenue; (4) the scale of its donor base (hundreds of institutional names, big corporate health funders). What does not transfer: the mix. DWC scaled *with* government and runs on 2–3 months of cash, which is the exposure Calvary is trying to reduce.

## California Black Women's Collective Empowerment Institute (runs SheWorks) — EIN 88-2046704, Carson CA, FYE June, founded 2022

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1.38M | $1.49M | $1.84M |
| Government share | 88% | 85% | 70% |
| Private contributions | $169K | $223K | $558K |
| Months of cash | 28.5 | 4.8 | 8.2 |

- A young research-and-advocacy institute (Black Women's Think Tank / Research and Innovation Institute, Conversations for Black Women, Protecting Black Women and Girls) that started almost entirely on government funding and is diversifying quickly; it also makes small grants ($42.5K to 8 organizations in TY2024).
- **SheWorks** was announced in October 2025 and runs its first cohort (about 30 women, January–May 2026) at the L.A. Care / Blue Shield Promise Community Resource Center in Long Beach, with stipends, childcare and ride credits; the LA County Federation of Labor is a partial funder and the health plans host in kind. No FAC or USAspending awards. Its funding will appear only in TY2025+ filings.
- **What transfers to Calvary:** the program design (cohort model, stipends, childcare and transport, employer and health-plan partners, system-impacted women as a named population) as an upgrade to Step Up DC; the partner structure (labor federation + health plans) as a corporate-partnership pattern. Not a funding model to copy: the parent is more government-dependent than Calvary.

## Caveats
DWC's fiscal year is the calendar year; line 1e is the government measure for both (no audits in the data except DWC's single audits for federal money); LA-area funders of DWC are only partly captured (web lists without amounts, DMV funders' filings only).

## Confidence
High on DWC's trajectory and federal programs; medium on funder lists; medium on the SheWorks parent (three filings, program launched after the last one).
